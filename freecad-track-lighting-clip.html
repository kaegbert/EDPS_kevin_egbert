FreeCAD track lighting clip

A parametric clip modeled in FreeCAD with help from Codex computer use. I described the part in inches, asked Codex to drive FreeCAD on my machine, and iterated until the geometry matched the rail and boss dimensions below.

What it is

A small track-lighting clip: twin rails with a centered boss, an entry gap for sliding onto the track, an internal cavity, and an axial through-hole. Uniform arm thickness keeps the piece light while the bridge ties the rails together along the length.

How I used Codex computer use

Instead of clicking every sketch and pad myself, I prompted Codex to operate FreeCAD directly — opening the document, building sketches, applying pads and pockets, and checking measurements against the table. When a dimension was off, I corrected the prompt with the exact inch and millimeter values and let Codex revise the model.

The prompt was essentially a feature checklist: rail length, width across arms, body and boss heights, cavity size, arm thickness, entry gap, bridge, and the centered through-hole. Codex translated that list into FreeCAD operations until the part matched.

Takeaway

Computer use let Codex act as a CAD operator while I stayed focused on the design intent. A clear dimension table turned into a reproducible FreeCAD part — and a documented prompt trail for how the clip was built.
